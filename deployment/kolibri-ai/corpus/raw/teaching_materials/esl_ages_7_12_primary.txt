English Teaching Guide for Primary Learners — Ages 7-12
Angels Academy Teaching Reference

OVERVIEW
Children ages 7-12 are in the primary stage of language learning. They can begin to read and write in English, understand basic grammar concepts, and engage in more structured activities. They are curious and eager to learn, but still need variety and engagement. Lesson activities should balance listening, speaking, reading, and writing. Attention spans are longer (10-20 minutes per activity) but children still benefit from movement and games.

DEVELOPMENTAL CHARACTERISTICS
Children ages 7-12:
- Are developing literacy skills in their first language
- Can understand and apply basic grammar rules
- Can read simple texts and write short sentences
- Have longer attention spans but still need variety
- Are developing logical thinking and problem-solving skills
- Begin to compare their language with English
- Can work in pairs and groups more effectively
- May become more self-conscious about making mistakes
- Are motivated by games, competition, and rewards
- Can begin to use dictionaries and reference materials

CEFR LEVEL TARGETS
Ages 7-9: Working toward A1 (Beginner)
Ages 10-12: Working toward A2 (Elementary)

GRAMMAR FOR AGES 7-9 (CEFR A1)

Present Simple Tense:
The present simple is used for habits, routines, and general truths.
Structure: Subject + base verb (add -s/-es for he/she/it)
Examples: I play football. She likes apples. We go to school every day.
Negative: I do not (don't) like spinach. He does not (doesn't) play tennis.
Questions: Do you like ice cream? Does she speak English?

Present Continuous Tense:
Used for actions happening right now.
Structure: Subject + am/is/are + verb-ing
Examples: I am reading a book. She is playing outside. They are eating lunch.
Questions: What are you doing? Is he sleeping?

Articles (a, an, the):
Use "a" before consonant sounds: a book, a cat, a house
Use "an" before vowel sounds: an apple, an egg, an umbrella
Use "the" for specific items: the teacher, the red ball

Possessives:
my, your, his, her, its, our, their
Examples: my book, your pencil, his mother, her cat, our school

Prepositions of Place:
in, on, under, next to, behind, in front of, between
Examples: The book is on the table. The cat is under the chair.

Can/Cannot for Ability:
I can swim. She can ride a bicycle. He cannot (can't) fly.
Questions: Can you play the piano? Can she speak French?

GRAMMAR FOR AGES 10-12 (CEFR A2)

Past Simple Tense:
Regular verbs: add -ed (walked, played, liked, wanted)
Irregular verbs: went, saw, had, made, took, gave, ate, drank, wrote, read, came, ran, said, told, thought, knew, found, got, bought
Structure: Subject + past form
Examples: I walked to school yesterday. She ate breakfast at 7 o'clock.
Negative: I did not (didn't) go to the park. She didn't see the movie.
Questions: Did you play football? Did she finish her homework?

Future with "going to" and "will":
Going to: for plans and intentions. I am going to visit my grandmother.
Will: for predictions and decisions. It will rain tomorrow. I will help you.

Comparatives and Superlatives:
Short adjectives: big → bigger → the biggest; tall → taller → the tallest
Long adjectives: beautiful → more beautiful → the most beautiful
Irregular: good → better → the best; bad → worse → the worst

Countable and Uncountable Nouns:
Countable: apple/apples, book/books, child/children
Uncountable: water, rice, milk, bread, homework, information
Use "some" and "any": There is some water. There aren't any apples.
Use "much" and "many": How much water? How many books?

Adverbs of Frequency:
always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never
Position: before the main verb, after "be"
Examples: I always brush my teeth. She is usually happy.

School Subjects:
English, mathematics, science, history, geography, art, music, physical education, computer science, religion

LESSON STRUCTURE FOR AGES 7-12

A typical 45-minute lesson:

1. Warm-up (5 minutes): Review game, quick questions, or a short activity to activate prior knowledge
2. Presentation (10 minutes): Introduce new language through a text, dialogue, or visual presentation
3. Controlled Practice (10 minutes): Structured activities where students practice the new language (gap-fill, matching, repetition drills)
4. Freer Practice (10 minutes): Activities where students use the language more freely (pair work, role play, information gap)
5. Production (7 minutes): Students create something using the language (write sentences, make a poster, do a presentation)
6. Wrap-up (3 minutes): Review key vocabulary, assign homework, preview next lesson

ACTIVITY IDEAS FOR AGES 7-12

1. Bingo: Create bingo cards with vocabulary items. Teacher calls out words and students mark them. First to complete a row shouts "Bingo!"

2. Running Dictation: Put a short text on the wall. Students work in pairs — one runs to read a sentence, runs back and dictates it to their partner who writes it down.

3. Information Gap: Each student has a picture with some missing information. They ask their partner questions to complete their picture without looking at each other's paper.

4. Board Race: Divide class into two teams. Write a question or category on the board. One student from each team races to write the correct answer.

5. Sentence Building: Give students cards with individual words. They work in groups to arrange them into correct sentences.

6. Story Chain: Each student adds one sentence to a story. "Once upon a time there was a girl. She lived in a village. One day she found a..." Continue around the class.

7. Vocabulary Mind Map: Write a topic in the center of the board. Students contribute related words, building a mind map together.

8. Role Play: Students act out real-life situations: ordering food at a restaurant, buying things at a market, visiting a doctor, meeting a new friend.

9. Reading Comprehension Race: Give groups a short text with questions. First group to correctly answer all questions wins.

10. Picture Description: Show a picture. Students take turns describing what they see. "I can see a boy. He is wearing a blue shirt. He is playing football."

READING SKILLS DEVELOPMENT

For Beginning Readers (Ages 7-8):
- Start with phonics: teach letter sounds systematically
- Use decodable texts with controlled vocabulary
- Read aloud to students regularly
- Use big books that the whole class can see
- Practice sight words (common words that don't follow phonics rules): the, is, was, said, have, come, they, what, where, there

For Developing Readers (Ages 9-10):
- Introduce reading strategies: prediction, making connections, asking questions
- Use graded readers at appropriate levels
- Practice skimming (reading quickly for main idea) and scanning (reading for specific information)
- Teach vocabulary in context
- Have students keep a reading log

For Independent Readers (Ages 11-12):
- Use authentic texts adapted for level
- Teach critical reading skills
- Introduce different text types: narratives, informational texts, letters, instructions
- Practice reading comprehension strategies: main idea, supporting details, inference, sequence
- Encourage extensive reading for pleasure

WRITING SKILLS DEVELOPMENT

Ages 7-8: Copying words and simple sentences, labeling pictures, completing sentences, writing lists
Ages 9-10: Writing simple sentences from prompts, short paragraphs (3-5 sentences), personal information, descriptions
Ages 11-12: Writing paragraphs with topic sentences, simple letters and messages, short stories (beginning, middle, end), comparing and contrasting

ASSESSMENT FOR AGES 7-12
- Use a mix of formal and informal assessment
- Include speaking, listening, reading, and writing tasks
- Use portfolio assessment to track progress over time
- Give constructive feedback, not just scores
- Use peer assessment and self-assessment activities
- Make tests fun where possible (e.g., quiz games)
- Focus on progress and effort, not just accuracy

TEACHING ENGLISH THROUGH BIBLE STORIES
Bible stories provide excellent material for English teaching. They are culturally relevant in the Angels Academy context and provide moral lessons alongside language learning.

Suggested Bible Stories for Language Teaching:
- Creation (Genesis 1-2): vocabulary for nature, animals, days of the week
- Noah's Ark: animal vocabulary, weather, colors (rainbow)
- David and Goliath: adjectives (big/small, brave/scared), past tense narrative
- The Good Samaritan: community vocabulary, helping others, past tense
- Daniel and the Lions: animal vocabulary, feelings (scared/brave/faithful)
- Jonah and the Whale: sea vocabulary, directions, feelings
- The Prodigal Son: family vocabulary, feelings, past tense narrative
- Jesus Feeds 5000: food vocabulary, numbers, sharing

Approach: Read the story simply, practice vocabulary, have students retell the story, do a related craft or activity, discuss the moral lesson.
