English Teaching Guide for Young Learners — Ages 3-6 (Pre-Primary)
Angels Academy Teaching Reference

OVERVIEW
Children ages 3-6 are in the pre-primary stage of language development. They learn best through play, songs, repetition, movement, and sensory experiences. At this age, the focus should be on listening and speaking rather than reading and writing. Children have short attention spans (5-10 minutes per activity) and need frequent changes of activity.

DEVELOPMENTAL CHARACTERISTICS
Children ages 3-6 have the following characteristics relevant to language learning:
- They learn through imitation and repetition
- They respond well to songs, chants, rhymes, and stories
- They need physical movement and hands-on activities
- They have limited attention spans (5-10 minutes per activity)
- They learn best when content is connected to their daily lives
- They cannot yet read or write fluently in their first language
- They are not self-conscious about making mistakes
- They respond well to praise and positive reinforcement
- They learn holistically rather than analytically
- They need routine and predictable lesson structures

LESSON STRUCTURE FOR AGES 3-6

A typical 30-minute lesson should follow this structure:

1. Warm-up (5 minutes): Greeting routine, hello song, review of previous lesson
2. Presentation (5 minutes): Introduce new vocabulary with flashcards, realia, or puppets
3. Practice Activity 1 (5 minutes): Song or chant with the new vocabulary
4. Movement Break (3 minutes): Total Physical Response (TPR) activity
5. Practice Activity 2 (5 minutes): Game or hands-on activity
6. Story Time (5 minutes): Short picture story incorporating vocabulary
7. Goodbye Routine (2 minutes): Goodbye song, sticker reward

TEACHING METHODS FOR AGES 3-6

Total Physical Response (TPR):
TPR is one of the most effective methods for teaching young children. The teacher gives commands and children respond physically. For example: "Stand up!" (children stand), "Touch your nose!" (children touch nose), "Jump!" (children jump). Start with simple commands and gradually add complexity. Always demonstrate the action first.

Songs and Chants:
Songs are essential for young learners. They help children learn pronunciation, rhythm, and vocabulary naturally. Use action songs where children can move while singing. Popular categories include: counting songs, body part songs, animal songs, weather songs, color songs, and greeting songs. Repeat songs frequently — children love repetition and learn through it.

Flashcard Activities:
Use large, colorful flashcards to present vocabulary. Activities include: show and say (hold up card, children repeat), what's missing (remove one card, children identify), memory game (match pairs), and point to (teacher says word, children point to correct card). Always use visuals — children at this age are visual learners.

Storytelling:
Use picture books with simple, repetitive language. Read the story multiple times over several lessons. Use different voices for characters. Ask children to point to objects in the pictures. Have children join in with repeated phrases. Use puppets or props to make the story come alive.

Crafts and Art:
Integrate language learning with craft activities. For example: coloring worksheets to practice colors, making animal masks to practice animal names, drawing family members to practice family vocabulary. Always narrate the activity in English: "Let's use the red crayon. Color the apple red."

ACTIVITY IDEAS FOR AGES 3-6

1. Simon Says: Practice body parts and actions. "Simon says touch your head!" Children follow only when the command starts with "Simon says."

2. Color Hunt: Ask children to find objects of a specific color in the classroom. "Find something blue!" Children bring back blue objects.

3. Animal Sounds: Make animal sounds and children guess the animal. Then reverse — show the animal picture and children make the sound.

4. Musical Flashcards: Pass flashcards around while playing music. When music stops, the child holding the card must say the word.

5. Ball Toss: Sit in a circle. Toss a ball to a child and ask a question. "What's your name?" The child answers and tosses the ball to another child.

6. Freeze Dance: Play music and children dance. When the music stops, show a flashcard and children say the word while frozen.

7. Picture Dictation: Teacher describes a simple scene and children draw it. "Draw a big sun. Draw a tree. Draw a red flower."

8. Sorting Games: Children sort objects by color, size, or category. Practice language while sorting: "This is big. This is small. Put the big ones here."

ASSESSMENT FOR AGES 3-6
Assessment at this age should be informal and observational. Look for:
- Can the child understand simple commands?
- Can the child repeat words and short phrases?
- Can the child identify vocabulary items when shown pictures?
- Does the child participate in songs and chants?
- Can the child respond to simple questions (What's this? What color is it?)

Do NOT use formal tests or written assessments for this age group.

CLASSROOM MANAGEMENT TIPS
- Use a consistent routine — children feel secure with predictability
- Give clear, simple instructions with gestures
- Use a reward system (stickers, stamps, stars)
- Transition between activities with songs or countdown
- Group children in pairs or small groups for activities
- Have extra activities ready in case you finish early
- Be patient — children need many repetitions to learn
- Celebrate effort, not just correct answers
- Use the child's first language sparingly for instructions when needed
- Create a safe, warm environment where children feel comfortable trying
