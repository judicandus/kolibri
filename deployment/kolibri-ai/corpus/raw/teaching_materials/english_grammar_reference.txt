English Grammar Reference for ESL Teachers
Angels Academy Teaching Reference

This reference guide covers the essential grammar points that ESL teachers need to explain to students at all levels. Use this as a quick reference when preparing lessons or answering student questions.

PARTS OF SPEECH

Nouns: Words that name people, places, things, or ideas.
Common nouns: teacher, school, book, happiness
Proper nouns: God, Jesus, Sabbath, Africa, Chad, Myanmar (always capitalized)
Countable nouns: book/books, child/children, person/people
Uncountable nouns: water, rice, information, advice, homework, furniture, weather
Collective nouns: family, class, team, group, church, congregation
Plural rules: add -s (cats), add -es after s/sh/ch/x (churches), change y to -ies (families), irregular (children, people, teeth, feet, mice)

Pronouns: Words that replace nouns.
Subject pronouns: I, you, he, she, it, we, they
Object pronouns: me, you, him, her, it, us, them
Possessive adjectives: my, your, his, her, its, our, their
Possessive pronouns: mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs
Reflexive pronouns: myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, themselves
Demonstrative pronouns: this, that, these, those
Relative pronouns: who, which, that, whose, where, when

Verbs: Words that express actions or states.
Action verbs: run, eat, read, teach, pray, sing, write
State verbs: be, have, know, like, love, believe, understand, want, need
Auxiliary verbs: be (am/is/are/was/were), have (have/has/had), do (do/does/did)
Modal verbs: can, could, may, might, will, would, shall, should, must, ought to

Adjectives: Words that describe nouns.
Position: before the noun (a big house) or after be (the house is big)
Order of adjectives: opinion-size-age-shape-color-origin-material-purpose
Example: a beautiful large old round brown Italian wooden dining table
Comparative: -er or more + adjective (bigger, more beautiful)
Superlative: -est or most + adjective (biggest, most beautiful)

Adverbs: Words that describe verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs.
Manner (how): slowly, quickly, carefully, well, badly
Frequency (how often): always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never
Time (when): yesterday, today, tomorrow, now, soon, already, yet
Place (where): here, there, everywhere, nowhere, outside, inside
Degree (how much): very, quite, really, extremely, fairly, rather

Prepositions: Words that show relationships between nouns.
Place: in, on, at, under, behind, between, next to, in front of, above, below
Time: at (at 3 o'clock), on (on Monday), in (in January, in 2026)
Movement: to, from, into, out of, through, across, along, toward
Other: for, with, without, about, by, of

Conjunctions: Words that connect words, phrases, or clauses.
Coordinating: and, but, or, so, yet, for, nor (FANBOYS)
Subordinating: because, although, if, when, while, before, after, until, since, unless
Correlative: both...and, either...or, neither...nor, not only...but also

VERB TENSES

Present Simple:
Form: base verb (I/you/we/they play) or base + s/es (he/she/it plays)
Use: habits, routines, facts, schedules
Signals: always, usually, often, sometimes, every day/week/month
Examples: I teach English. She goes to church on Sabbath. Water boils at 100 degrees.

Present Continuous:
Form: am/is/are + verb-ing
Use: actions happening now, temporary situations, future arrangements
Signals: now, at the moment, currently, right now, today
Examples: I am teaching a class right now. She is living in Chad this year.

Present Perfect:
Form: have/has + past participle
Use: experiences, recent events, situations continuing from past to present
Signals: ever, never, already, yet, just, for, since, so far
Examples: I have taught for ten years. She has never visited Thailand.

Present Perfect Continuous:
Form: have/has + been + verb-ing
Use: actions that started in the past and continue to the present
Signals: for, since, all day, all morning
Examples: I have been teaching since 8 o'clock. They have been waiting for an hour.

Past Simple:
Form: base + ed (regular) or irregular form
Use: completed actions in the past
Signals: yesterday, last week, in 2020, ago, when I was young
Examples: I visited Myanmar last year. She taught at the school for five years.
Common irregular verbs: be-was/were, go-went, come-came, see-saw, do-did, have-had, make-made, take-took, give-gave, say-said, tell-told, think-thought, know-knew, get-got, find-found, write-wrote, read-read, speak-spoke, eat-ate, drink-drank, run-ran, begin-began, break-broke, choose-chose, draw-drew, drive-drove, fall-fell, feel-felt, fly-flew, forget-forgot, grow-grew, hide-hid, hold-held, keep-kept, leave-left, lose-lost, meet-met, pay-paid, put-put, ride-rode, ring-rang, rise-rose, sell-sold, send-sent, set-set, show-showed, shut-shut, sing-sang, sit-sat, sleep-slept, spend-spent, stand-stood, steal-stole, swim-swam, teach-taught, throw-threw, understand-understood, wake-woke, wear-wore, win-won

Past Continuous:
Form: was/were + verb-ing
Use: ongoing action in the past, background action
Examples: I was reading when the phone rang. While she was cooking, the children were playing.

Past Perfect:
Form: had + past participle
Use: action completed before another past action
Examples: By the time I arrived, the service had already started. She had finished her homework before dinner.

Future with Will:
Form: will + base verb
Use: predictions, spontaneous decisions, promises, offers
Examples: It will rain tomorrow. I will help you. She will become a good teacher.

Future with Going To:
Form: am/is/are + going to + base verb
Use: plans, intentions, predictions based on evidence
Examples: I am going to study medicine. Look at those clouds — it is going to rain.

SENTENCE STRUCTURE

Simple Sentence: Subject + Verb + (Object)
The teacher reads a book.

Compound Sentence: Two independent clauses joined by a conjunction
The teacher reads a book, and the students listen.

Complex Sentence: Independent clause + dependent clause
The teacher reads a book because the students enjoy stories.
When the bell rings, the students go home.

Questions:
Yes/No questions: Do you like English? Is she a teacher? Can you swim?
Wh- questions: What is your name? Where do you live? When does school start? Why are you late? How do you say this in English? How many students are there? How much does it cost?

COMMON ERRORS AND HOW TO CORRECT THEM

Subject-Verb Agreement:
Wrong: She don't like fish. Correct: She doesn't like fish.
Wrong: The children is playing. Correct: The children are playing.

Article Usage:
Wrong: I am teacher. Correct: I am a teacher.
Wrong: She went to the home. Correct: She went home.
Wrong: I like the music. (general) Correct: I like music.

Tense Consistency:
Wrong: Yesterday I go to school and eat lunch. Correct: Yesterday I went to school and ate lunch.

Word Order:
Wrong: I like very much English. Correct: I like English very much.
Wrong: She speaks well English. Correct: She speaks English well.

Preposition Errors:
Wrong: I arrived to school. Correct: I arrived at school.
Wrong: She is married with him. Correct: She is married to him.
Wrong: I am interested for science. Correct: I am interested in science.

Double Negatives:
Wrong: I don't have no money. Correct: I don't have any money.

Confusing Words:
teach vs. learn: The teacher teaches. The student learns.
make vs. do: make breakfast, make a mistake / do homework, do exercise
say vs. tell: She said hello. She told me a story.
borrow vs. lend: Can I borrow your pen? Can you lend me your pen?
come vs. go: Come here (toward speaker). Go there (away from speaker).
bring vs. take: Bring it here. Take it there.
much vs. many: much water (uncountable), many books (countable)
few vs. little: few friends (countable), little time (uncountable)
good vs. well: She is a good teacher. She teaches well.
its vs. it's: The dog wagged its tail. It's raining. (it is)
there/their/they're: There is a book. Their book. They're reading.

PRONUNCIATION GUIDE FOR ESL TEACHERS

English Vowel Sounds:
Short vowels: /æ/ cat, /ɛ/ bed, /ɪ/ sit, /ɒ/ hot, /ʌ/ cup, /ʊ/ book
Long vowels: /iː/ see, /ɑː/ car, /ɔː/ door, /uː/ food, /ɜː/ bird
Diphthongs: /eɪ/ day, /aɪ/ my, /ɔɪ/ boy, /aʊ/ how, /əʊ/ go, /ɪə/ here, /eə/ there

Consonant Sounds That May Be Difficult:
/θ/ think, thank — tongue between teeth, air flows out
/ð/ this, the, that — like /θ/ but with voice
/r/ red, run — tongue does not touch the roof of the mouth
/l/ like, love — tongue touches behind top teeth
/v/ very — top teeth touch bottom lip with voice
/w/ water, we — round lips, then open

Word Stress Rules:
Two-syllable nouns: stress usually on first syllable (TEAcher, STUdent, TAble)
Two-syllable verbs: stress usually on second syllable (beLIEVE, beCOME, forGET)
Words ending in -tion/-sion: stress on syllable before (eduCAtion, informAtion)
Words ending in -ic: stress on syllable before (fanTAStic, draMAtic)

Sentence Stress:
Content words (nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs) are stressed
Function words (articles, prepositions, auxiliaries) are usually unstressed
Example: I WANT to GO to SCHOOL toMORrow.

Intonation:
Rising intonation (↗): yes/no questions (Do you like English↗?)
Falling intonation (↘): statements and wh- questions (Where do you live↘?)
