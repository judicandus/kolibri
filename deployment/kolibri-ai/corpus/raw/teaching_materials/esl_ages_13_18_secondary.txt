English Teaching Guide for Secondary Learners — Ages 13-18
Angels Academy Teaching Reference

OVERVIEW
Teenagers ages 13-18 are in the secondary stage of language learning. They can handle more complex grammar, longer texts, and abstract topics. They are developing critical thinking skills and can engage in discussions, debates, and project-based learning. However, they can also be self-conscious, easily bored, and resistant to activities they perceive as childish. Lessons must be relevant, engaging, and respectful of their growing maturity.

DEVELOPMENTAL CHARACTERISTICS
Teenagers ages 13-18:
- Have well-developed literacy in their first language
- Can understand and use complex grammar structures
- Can read longer texts and write extended compositions
- Have strong opinions and enjoy expressing them
- Are sensitive to peer opinion and may fear making mistakes
- Are motivated by topics relevant to their lives and interests
- Can work independently and in collaborative projects
- Respond well to technology and multimedia
- Need to see the practical purpose of what they are learning
- Are developing identity and may use language as self-expression

CEFR LEVEL TARGETS
Ages 13-15: Working toward B1 (Intermediate)
Ages 16-18: Working toward B2 (Upper-Intermediate)

GRAMMAR FOR AGES 13-15 (CEFR B1)

Present Perfect Tense:
Used for past actions with present relevance or experiences.
Structure: Subject + have/has + past participle
Examples: I have visited three countries. She has lived here for five years. They have never eaten sushi.
Keywords: already, yet, just, ever, never, for, since
Difference from Past Simple: "I visited Paris in 2024" (specific time) vs. "I have visited Paris" (experience, time not specified)

Past Continuous Tense:
Used for ongoing actions in the past, often interrupted by another action.
Structure: Subject + was/were + verb-ing
Examples: I was reading when the phone rang. They were playing football at 3 o'clock yesterday.
Often used with Past Simple: While I was cooking, she arrived.

First Conditional:
For real/possible future situations.
Structure: If + present simple, will + base verb
Examples: If it rains, we will stay home. If you study hard, you will pass the exam.

Second Conditional:
For unreal/hypothetical situations.
Structure: If + past simple, would + base verb
Examples: If I had a million dollars, I would travel the world. If she were president, she would build more schools.

Passive Voice (present and past):
Structure: Subject + be + past participle
Present: The book is read by many students. English is spoken in many countries.
Past: The church was built in 1950. The letters were written by Paul.

Relative Clauses:
Who (for people): The teacher who taught me English was very kind.
Which/That (for things): The book which I read was interesting.
Where (for places): The school where I study is near the church.

Modal Verbs for Advice and Obligation:
Should/shouldn't: You should study every day. You shouldn't skip breakfast.
Must/mustn't: You must wear a uniform. You mustn't cheat on exams.
Have to/don't have to: I have to do homework. You don't have to come early.

GRAMMAR FOR AGES 16-18 (CEFR B2)

Past Perfect Tense:
For actions completed before another past action.
Structure: Subject + had + past participle
Examples: By the time I arrived, the class had already started. She had never seen snow before she visited Canada.

Third Conditional:
For unreal past situations (things that didn't happen).
Structure: If + past perfect, would have + past participle
Examples: If I had studied harder, I would have passed. If they had left earlier, they wouldn't have missed the bus.

Reported Speech:
Direct: She said, "I am tired."
Reported: She said that she was tired.
Tense changes: am→was, will→would, can→could, present→past
Questions: He asked if I was coming. She asked where I lived.

Gerunds and Infinitives:
Gerund (verb + -ing): I enjoy reading. She avoids eating sugar. Swimming is good exercise.
Infinitive (to + verb): I want to learn English. She decided to become a teacher.
Some verbs take gerund: enjoy, avoid, suggest, finish, mind, practice
Some verbs take infinitive: want, decide, hope, plan, agree, promise

Phrasal Verbs:
Common phrasal verbs: look after (care for), give up (stop), put off (delay), turn on/off, pick up, find out, carry on (continue), look forward to, get along with, come up with (think of)

Advanced Connectors:
Addition: furthermore, moreover, in addition
Contrast: however, nevertheless, on the other hand, although, despite
Cause/Effect: therefore, consequently, as a result, due to
Purpose: in order to, so that, so as to
Example: for instance, such as, for example

LESSON STRUCTURE FOR AGES 13-18

A typical 50-60 minute lesson:

1. Lead-in (5 minutes): Discussion question, image, or short video to introduce the topic and activate prior knowledge
2. Input (10-15 minutes): Reading text, listening activity, or presentation of new language in context
3. Language Focus (10 minutes): Analysis of grammar/vocabulary from the input, with clear examples and practice
4. Communicative Practice (15 minutes): Pair/group activities requiring use of the target language: discussions, debates, role plays, problem-solving tasks
5. Production (10 minutes): Individual or group output: writing a paragraph, preparing a presentation, creating a dialogue
6. Reflection (5 minutes): What did we learn? What was difficult? Homework assignment

ACTIVITY IDEAS FOR AGES 13-18

1. Debate: Divide class into two groups on a topic (e.g., "Technology helps education" vs. "Technology harms education"). Each side prepares arguments and presents them.

2. Jigsaw Reading: Different groups read different parts of a text. They then form new groups where each member shares their section, building a complete understanding.

3. Problem-Based Learning: Present a real-world problem (e.g., "Our village needs clean water"). Groups research, discuss, and present a solution in English.

4. News Report: Students create a news report (written or spoken) about a local or global event. Practice journalistic writing and presentation skills.

5. Interview Project: Students prepare questions and interview each other (or community members) about a topic. They then report their findings to the class.

6. Film/Video Discussion: Watch a short clip and discuss themes, characters, vocabulary, and cultural elements.

7. Creative Writing: Write a short story, poem, or personal essay. Share with classmates for peer feedback.

8. Formal Letter Writing: Practice writing formal letters for different purposes: requesting information, making complaints, applying for positions.

9. Presentation Skills: Students research a topic and give a 3-5 minute presentation to the class. Focus on structure, clarity, and confidence.

10. Discussion Circles: Small groups discuss a question (e.g., "What makes a good leader?"). Each person has a role: discussion leader, note-taker, reporter, timekeeper.

READING SKILLS FOR SECONDARY

Text Types to Practice:
- News articles and current events
- Short stories and excerpts from novels
- Informational texts (science, history, geography)
- Letters (formal and informal)
- Advertisements and persuasive texts
- Instructions and procedures
- Opinion pieces and editorials
- Biblical passages and devotional readings

Reading Strategies:
- Predicting: Using titles, images, and prior knowledge to predict content
- Skimming: Reading quickly for the main idea
- Scanning: Looking for specific information
- Inferring: Understanding meaning not directly stated
- Summarizing: Condensing main ideas into brief form
- Evaluating: Assessing the quality, reliability, and bias of a text
- Connecting: Relating text to personal experience, other texts, or world knowledge

WRITING SKILLS FOR SECONDARY

Types of Writing to Practice:
Ages 13-15: Descriptions, narratives, informal letters/emails, short reports, diary entries, reviews
Ages 16-18: Formal letters, essays (argumentative, discursive, compare/contrast), reports, proposals, summaries, creative writing

Essay Structure:
Introduction: Hook + background + thesis statement
Body Paragraphs: Topic sentence + supporting details + examples + concluding sentence
Conclusion: Restate thesis + summary of main points + final thought

Writing Process:
1. Brainstorm: Generate ideas
2. Plan/Outline: Organize ideas logically
3. Draft: Write a first version
4. Revise: Improve content and organization
5. Edit: Correct grammar, spelling, punctuation
6. Publish: Share the final version

SPEAKING SKILLS DEVELOPMENT
- Encourage fluency over accuracy in communicative activities
- Provide speaking frameworks and sentence starters
- Use think-pair-share to reduce anxiety
- Record and self-evaluate speaking activities
- Practice pronunciation through tongue twisters, minimal pairs, and shadowing
- Teach discourse markers: well, so, actually, basically, I mean, you know
- Practice different registers: formal vs. informal speech

ASSESSMENT FOR AGES 13-18
- Use rubrics for writing and speaking assessments
- Include self-assessment and peer assessment
- Balance formative (ongoing) and summative (end of unit) assessment
- Test all four skills: listening, speaking, reading, writing
- Use authentic tasks where possible (e.g., writing a real letter, giving a real presentation)
- Provide detailed, constructive feedback
- Allow students to revise and improve their work

TEACHING ENGLISH THROUGH ADVENTIST VALUES
For teenage students, connect English learning with values and real-world application:
- Discuss Sabbath-keeping vocabulary and expressions in English
- Read and discuss EGW quotes about education, character, and service
- Practice writing about personal faith journeys
- Debate ethical topics from a Christian perspective
- Study vocabulary related to mission work and community service
- Write letters to pen pals in other Angels Academy locations
- Present on the 28 Fundamental Beliefs using academic English
- Discuss health principles using health and nutrition vocabulary
